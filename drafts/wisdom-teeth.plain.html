What To Eat After Wisdom Teeth Removal

If you're preparing for an upcoming wisdom teeth removal surgery, you likely have questions about the surgery and recovery process, including what you'll be able to eat afterward. Understanding post-surgical care and the best post-surgery diet is a key component to making your recovery as quick and seamless as possible.

For people between the ages of 17 and 25, wisdom teeth — the third molars at the back of the mouth — often grow in impacted or at an angle, causing issues that may require extraction. The American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons (AAOMS) recommends removal when there is active infection, irreparable decay, or damage to neighboring teeth.

Immediately After Removal

Once your wisdom teeth have been removed, you'll leave with wet gauze pressed against the extraction site to stimulate clotting and slow bleeding. This clot will morph into healing tissue, filling the hole in your gum and bone.

To avoid a dry socket — which can prolong healing and be quite uncomfortable — take care not to disrupt the clot. Avoid sucking motions such as drinking through a straw, and avoid smoking, as these can dislodge the healing clot.

What to Avoid

While your mouth is still numb from the anesthetic, avoid hot foods and drinks to prevent burning. Steer clear of chewy foods, hard or crunchy snacks like chips and hard pretzels, small sharp foods like popcorn with hulls, and spicy foods that could cause gum sensitivity or stomach upset.

What to Eat

Nutrition is a key part of the reparative process following dental surgery. Along with drinking plenty of water, eat foods that are both nutritious and gentle on your mouth. The following foods provide important vitamins and nutrients while being easy to chew and swallow:

  • Yogurt
  • Cottage cheese
  • Apple sauce
  • Avocados
  • Fruit smoothies (seedless fruit)
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Soft fish
  • Finely cut meats
  • Mashed potatoes
  • Oatmeal
  • Thin soups

Stick to these items for four to seven days following oral surgery. Stock up before the procedure and avoid processed foods or items with added sugars.

Keep It Clean

Gentle cleansing and rinsing as directed by your dental professional will be an important part of your healing process. Choose a soft-bristled toothbrush designed to clean gently between teeth and along the gumline without disturbing the extraction site.
